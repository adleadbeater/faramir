# Increment 10 review: The Numbers chart parser

Branch `v2/phase-0`, local only. Nothing pushed, deployed, or sent to Slack or Sheets. **No new fetching:** the parser turns HTML into a validated chart. Nothing calls it on a schedule, there is no historical backfill, and production collection is unchanged.

## What was built

`src/faramir/tn_chart.py` is new and parse-only. It has no `requests` import, and a test enforces that.

- **`weekend_friday(d)`, `weekend_url(friday)`, `daily_url(d)`.** Weekend charts are keyed by their **Friday**. `weekend_url` refuses any date that isn't a Friday, because the Sunday URL returns 404 on TN.
- **`parse_chart(html, source_url, expected_type, expected_date)` returns a `Chart`.** It never raises on page content. The `Chart` carries:
  - `chart_type` and `reporting_date`, plus `weekend_start`/`weekend_end` for a weekend chart;
  - `source_url`, `rows`;
  - the footer's `reported_count`/`reported_total`, and `parsed_count`/`parsed_total`;
  - `issues`, `header`, `parser_version`, `status`, `verified`, and `content_hash()`.
- **Each `ChartRow` keeps the reported values:**
  - title and TN slug, slug year;
  - `source_rank` (exactly as TN printed it; `None` where TN shows `-`) alongside **`derived_rank`** (Faramir's rank by gross, with ties sharing, labelled as derived);
  - prev rank and its status (`ranked`, `new`, `unranked`, `missing`, `malformed`);
  - gross, daily and weekly change %, theaters, theater average, total gross, days in release;
  - the raw cell text.

Layout handling follows the 2026-10-16 weekend and 2026-10-21 daily captures:

- The chart type and date come from the `<h1>`.
- Only the `chart-desktop` table is read. The `chart-mobile` twin is ignored, and when there are several unmarked tables the parser refuses to guess.
- Columns are mapped by exact header (whitespace and `<br>` normalized), so reordered columns are fine.
- Unknown, missing, duplicate or out-of-place columns (such as "Daily Change" on a weekend chart) are errors.

## Classification and completeness

`complete` is the only status that is `verified`. Everything else is visible but must never be treated as verified data.

| Status | Raised by |
|---|---|
| `wrong_chart` | Page type or date differs from what was requested, for example a daily page fetched as a weekend |
| `malformed` | **Layout:** unrecognized heading; weekend heading not dated a Friday; unknown, missing, duplicate or unexpected column; no chart table.<br>**Values:** malformed number, malformed rank or prev cell; total gross below gross.<br>**Consistency:** rank sequence not 1..k-then-`-`, or printed ranks out of gross order; more rows than the footer reports; grosses that don't sum to the footer total when the row count matches, or that sum *above* it. |
| `partial` | A required value is missing (gross, theaters, total gross, days, title link); fewer rows than the footer reports, with the shortfall consistent (`gross_short`); no footer, so completeness can't be verified |
| `complete` | None of the above. Every required value parsed, ranks valid, row count equals the footer count, and the grosses sum **exactly** to the footer total. |

**A missing or malformed value is `None` plus an issue, never zero and never filled in.** For a new film, a blank change cell is `None`; that is legitimate and not an issue.

## Re-release evidence (kept for Discovery, excluded from Phase 0 picks)

`identity.classify_engagement(slug_year, days, chart_end)` assigns each row one of four classes, using both the slug year and TN's day count:

| Class | Evidence | Example | Total gross covers |
|---|---|---|---|
| `rerelease` | Still charting more than 365 days [CAL] after its first US release, or, with no day count, an old slug year | `Avengers-Endgame-(2019)` at 2,733 days | `all_engagements` (lifetime) |
| `late_release` | An older film in its first US run | `Fanfare-En-(2024-France)` at 59 days | `this_engagement` |
| `original` | First run, around its slug year | `Night-Ferry-(2026-United-Kingdom)` | `this_engagement` |
| `unknown` | Not enough evidence | — | `unknown` |

Each row keeps the **original film's identity** (the TN slug) separately from **this engagement**:

- `first_us_release` (implied by the day count);
- `engagement_start`: the same date for a first run; `None` for a re-release, because no single chart shows when a re-release began. Weekend history in Increment 11 can establish it, from the first weekend a film reappears after an absence.
- `total_gross_scope`.

In the daily run:

- The **Phase 0 exclusion is unchanged.** The slug-year rule still excludes those rows, and the identity report now records `engagement` and an `identity_class` of `rerelease`.
- **Genuine re-releases never reach identity resolution.** Their day counts are far above the 90-day active rule. They are now recorded, report-only, under `report.json → rerelease_engagements` (slug, slug year, days, lifetime total). They are never resolved, tracked or picked.
- **Consequence:** in production, the slug-year exclusion in practice catches *late releases*, not re-releases. Whether to keep excluding them is recorded as **PR-14**, for your decision. The offline fixture's "Old Classic (1985)" (synthetic, 12 days) is now labelled `late_release`, and it is still excluded.

## Identity confidence

- `Identity.identity_class` is `verified` (search), `manual` (override), `legacy`, `unresolved` or `rerelease`. The run report records it for every chart row.
- **A legacy match is never upgraded.** Run 1 matches Beta Story by title and year and backfills its slug. Run 2 matches it by that slug and keeps `identity_confidence = legacy` and method `state_legacy_title_year`. This is tested across two consecutive runs.
- Ongoing verification of legacy rows is recorded as **SR-6**.

## Weekend data is never replaced by daily data

- **The new parser:** a daily page requested as a weekend is `wrong_chart` and never verified. There is no fallback path in the module.
- **The legacy `fetch_weekend_list`** is unchanged, because changing it would change production collection.
  - Its docstring now states that it always returns **Sunday's daily chart** (the Sunday weekend URL returns 404).
  - Production's Monday merge, which uses it only to add missing titles, now records `monday_merge: {source: "sunday_daily_chart_via_legacy_fetch_weekend_list", is_weekend_data: false}`. A Monday test pins this, including the unchanged Sunday URL.
  - Replacing it is **PR-15**, at activation of history collection.

## Remaining risks

- **The 365-day revival threshold is a calibration value [CAL].** A genuinely long first run (an IMAX documentary running for over a year) would be labelled `rerelease`. That only affects the evidence label, never picks.
- **TN may change its markup.** Any header or heading change makes the chart `malformed`, never silently mis-mapped. The result is that no data is verified until the parser is updated.
- **Exact gross reconciliation** assumes TN's footer is the exact sum of the listed rows, which is true on both captures. If TN ever rounds the footer, every chart would read `malformed`. That would fail safe and be visible.
- **Theaters are required.** A chart with a legitimately blank theater count for a tiny release would be `partial`. That has not been seen in the captures.
- **Only two real pages were checked.** Holiday weekends (e.g. a Monday holiday), the 4-day-weekend layout and very short charts are untested on real data.
- **`late_release` films stay excluded from picks** until PR-14 is decided.

## Next: Increment 11 — weekend-history transaction protocol (collection off)

**What it implements.** `src/faramir/history.py`, the §5.2 protocol, writing to two append-only tabs: `weekend_history` (one row per film per collection) and `weekend_collections` (the event log).

1. **Only a `verified` (complete) `Chart` is collected.**
   - Any other status appends a single `reject` event, with the status and issues, and **writes no history rows**. This is test H9.
2. **Deterministic IDs:**
   - `collection_id` = `sha1(source | weekend_start | chart.content_hash())`;
   - `record_id` = `sha1(collection_id | tn_slug)`;
   - retries reuse both, because the content hash does not depend on time or URL.
3. **Each history row is a parsed `ChartRow`:**
   - the source rank and derived rank kept separate;
   - prev rank, gross, change %, theaters, average, total, days;
   - slug and slug year;
   - engagement class, first US release and total-gross scope (SR-5);
   - identity fields (`tmdb_id`, `identity_confidence`) where a state row matches by slug, never by title (SR-6).
4. **The transaction protocol:**
   - begin, then one `append_rows`, then a read-back of distinct `record_id`s, then commit;
   - resume after a crash;
   - revisions when the content changes;
   - readers use only committed rows, de-duplicated;
   - 48-hour orphan abort;
   - the append-only rule enforced by a test.
5. **Provisional flag:** a weekend stays provisional until a commit captured on or after the following Tuesday.
6. **Tests H1–H12** run on the fake gspread, with synthetic charts from `tn_synth.py`.

**What stays off:**

- `FARAMIR_COLLECT_HISTORY` defaults to false. Nothing fetches TN weekend pages unless it is set, and it is not set anywhere.
- No workflow change and no backfill.
- The two tabs are created only by the Increment 12 migration, at deployment, with approval, after the source-permission decision.
- The protocol is exercised only on fixtures.

**How it depends on this parser.** Increment 11 trusts only `Chart.verified`. Its IDs come from `content_hash()`. It stores the parser's reported and derived ranks separately. Its rows carry the parser's engagement evidence, and its `reject` events record the parser's `status` and `issues`. If the parser's classification were wrong, history would either store unverified data or reject good weekends. That is why Increment 10 is gated first.
