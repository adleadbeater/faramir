# Requirements carried into the Faramir 2.0 strategy engine

Recorded during Phase 0 review. Phase 0 keeps these claims blocked; the strategy engine (Phase 1+) must deliver them **with evidence**, not by loosening the Phase 0 validator.

| # | Requirement | Recorded |
|---|---|---|
| SR-1 | **Headline validation must support verified records, rankings and superlatives once the strategy engine supplies the evidence** (re-affirmed in the Increment 9 review). **Rankings, records, historical achievements and time-based headlines must become possible.** Faramir 2.0 needs legitimate claims such as "4th-Biggest Horror Opening", "First Film Since 2019 to…", "Biggest Second Weekend of the Year". Each needs a ranked-evidence source (e.g. weekend history, genre rankings) and a claim type that validation can check against that evidence. Until then the `rank`, `record`, `superlative`, `chart_position`, `first_or_only` and `count` claim types and their lexical patterns stay blocked. | Increment 8 review |
| SR-2 | **The validator must distinguish unsupported claims from ordinary language.** "In its first weekend", "only two weeks in", "only $3M away", "ever since", "most of its gross", "leading man", "at first" are ordinary language and pass. `tests/test_validation.py::LEGITIMATE` is a regression suite: any change that rejects one of these phrases makes the title engine too restrictive and must not merge. New legitimate phrasings found in the PR-12 replay are added to it. | Increment 8 review |
| SR-3 | **Genre claims must eventually identify, explicitly, the film they describe** (re-affirmed in the Increment 9 review). **Genre descriptors must be attributed to the right film,** not rejected because the genre belongs to the comparison film. Phase 0 attributes lexically (title adjacency, apposition, verb side; see `validate.genre_checks`). Proposed for a later prompt change (needs approval): a `genre_descriptor` claim states the film it describes via `comp_tmdb_id` (null = active film), so the declared claim is attributed too, not only the wording. | Increment 8 review |
| SR-4 | **Rewrites never change meaning.** Any automated rewrite (shortening today; style or reframing later) keeps the same films, the same film doing the passing, the same milestone status and the same figures, and is re-validated in full (`validate.meaning_changes`). | Increment 8 review |
| SR-5 | **Re-release data must remain available for Discovery.** Phase 0 picks exclude re-releases, but their evidence is kept: the TN slug (the original film's identity), slug year, TN days in release, the first US release date implied by it, the engagement class (`original`, `late_release`, `rerelease`, `unknown`) and the scope of the total gross (`all_engagements` for a re-release). `tn_chart.ChartRow` and `identity.Identity.engagement` carry these; weekend history (Increment 11) must store them. The start of a re-release engagement is not on any single chart (TN's day count runs from the first US release); it has to come from history (the first weekend a film reappears after an absence). Discovery and any re-release strategy build on this; Phase 0 does not implement them. | Increment 9 review |
| SR-6 | **Legacy identity matches need ongoing verification.** Rows matched by pre-Increment-9 title and year carry `identity_confidence = legacy` and keep it on every later run, including slug matches; a chart parser or title match never upgrades them. A later verification pass (TMDB `/find` by IMDb ID, release year against slug year, release window) may promote a row to verified, recorded with its own method; until then, legacy identities are reported as such and franchise/creative angles should treat them as lower confidence. | Increment 9 review |
