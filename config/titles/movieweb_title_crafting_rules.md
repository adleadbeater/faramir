# MovieWeb Title Crafting Rules

Sep 28, 2026 · @Rob

## How to use this

These rules come from MovieWeb and Screen-network Cerebro data, Mar 1 – Sep 14 2026. Every figure below was validated against the specific desk it applies to, not pulled from the Power Words sheet.

The rules are ordered by how often they change an outcome. Rules 1 and 2 are process — get them wrong and every number that follows is wrong. Rules 3 to 6 are the craft. Rule 7 overrides all of them.

Refresh the figures quarterly. Phrases decay: "Stephen King-Approved" was a working modifier before it became boilerplate at 65–256 sessions per story.

## 1. Scope the baseline first

A phrase is only strong or weak relative to the right comparison set. Niche News inflates the News-desk picture, so the same phrase can look strong at one scope and fail at another.

| Scope | S/A | FR% | DV% | DC/A |
| --- | --: | --: | --: | --: |
| MW News + Niche News | 14,344 | 17.9 | 58.7 | 9,865 |
| MW News + Flash News only | 8,206 | 21.5 | 54.5 | 5,368 |
| MW Reviews | 1,214 | 49.6 | 30.7 | — |
| Intent = Brand (exclusives) | 4,696 | 38.4 | 51.4 | — |
| Network (MW + SR + CBR + CL) | \~10,500 | — | — | — |

Judge a News title against 8,206, not 14,344. "One of the Biggest" reads as a top phrase at the wider scope and sits below baseline at 5,866 on News + Flash.

## 2. Validate before recommending

Three checks. Each caught a real error during the session that produced this doc.

**The Power Words sheet runs hot.** It pools Niche News streaming stories, so it overstates News-desk performance by roughly 1.5–4×.

| Phrase | Sheet S/A | Actual on MW News + Flash |
| --- | --: | --: |
| One of the Biggest | 77,700 | 5,866 |
| Thriller Masterpiece | 40,253 | 10,846 |
| Becomes | 30,014 | 16,148 |
| Officially | 23,626 | 11,996 |
| Masterpiece | 21,407 | 12,770 |
| Near-Perfect (in RT stories) | 19,639 | 6,462 |

Use the sheet as a shortlist. Validate the number against the desk before it goes in a recommendation.

**Check for keyword contamination.** Four caught here: "Cut" at 36,389 was *Cut From Netflix*; "consecutive / in a row" at 41,943 was *straight-to-streaming*; "Blasts" was *Blasts Onto Streaming*; "Supernatural" matched the genre word, not the show. If a cut looks surprisingly strong, print the titles before trusting it.

**Check the exact phrase, not the parent word.** They diverge constantly.

| Parent | Exact phrase | Change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Update 6,880 | Major Update 4,889 → Promising Update 3,892 | falls |
| Milestone 19,269 | Crosses Milestone 7,846 | falls |
| Addresses 6,116 | Officially Addresses 10,447 | rises |
| Joins 4,227 | Officially Joins 16,871 | rises |

## 3. The architecture

Winning titles follow one shape:

**Subject the reader already cares about → genre descriptor → strong verb → concrete payload.**

Every element is replaceable except the last.

**Vagueness is the dominant failure mode.** These all failed for the same reason — the reader can't picture what happened:

- "Major Milestone" on a production stage: 222–6,059 range
- "One of the Biggest Mistakes"
- "Iconic Wizarding Lore" (the story was about wands)
- "Many Decisions"
- "Future of Jon Snow Spin-Off Revival"

The winners in the same categories all named the thing: the time jump, the exit, the lineup, the Lockhart recast, the 1997 setting.

Corollary: if a source's own headline is vague, that's the thing to fix, not to copy.

## 5. Verbs and the "Officially" effect

**"Officially" is the highest-value single word available.** 568 uses on the News desk, and it separates outcomes sharply:

- Snyder's Netflix return *with* it: 23,130. His MMA project *without*: 372.
- Casting, additive + Officially: 16,871 (0% failure, 100% DV). Additive alone: 3,599.
- Recast + Officially: 28,289. Recast alone: 9,638.

It costs one word. When trimming to the character cap, cut anything else first.

| Verb construction | S/A | Note |
| --- | --: | --- |
| Officially Casts / Recasts | 32,311 | needs a real event |
| Officially Announces | 31,754 | needs a real event |
| Sets New Release | 29,828 | 3.2% FR — physical media |
| Officially Moving Forward | 21,246 | 0% FR, but implies a greenlight |
| Officially Returns | 17,897 |  |
| Officially Addresses | 10,447 | reliable floor for quote stories |
| Confirms | 6,333 |  |
| Gives Update / Update On | 3,306 |  |
| Reveals | 3,508 |  |

**"Officially Addresses" only works when the object is a property with stakes.** Marvel on Blade's fate 42,163 · De Niro on a recast 29,977 · Lucasfilm on the Star Wars slate 26,520. Against: Dwayne Johnson on his own Oscar snub 343 · Netflix on pricing 713 · Cameron on his own career 1,431.

The speaker's fame does not rescue a weak object. Johnson and Cameron are bigger names than the ones above them.

## 6. Structural findings

**Slot-change beats addition.** Recast or replacement casting runs 16,812 S/A at 7.7% failure; additive "joins" runs 5,520 at 27.6%. Readers care about the role, not the job. The failures are all "X joins Y project" — Gosling joining a sci-fi film did 118 sessions.

**"X Meets Y" is the strongest construction on the site.** 129 uses, 35,256 S/A, 86.8% DV, 28,173 DC/A. It's an editorial comparison, not a sourced quote.

**Quoted comparison is not quoted speech.** Quote-marked titles look fine in aggregate at 15,910 until you split them:

| Type | Uses | S/A | FR% |
| --- | --: | --: | --: |
| Quoted "X Meets Y" comparison | 76 | 38,438 | 10.5 |
| Quoted, neither | 246 | 12,877 | 18.3 |
| Quoted actual speech | 83 | 4,200 | 30.1 |

When you must quote speech, use one or two words alongside a hard fact — the Bale ("Exciting" role, 38,588) and Bosch ("Proper Closure", 20,113) pattern. Never a quoted sentence as the whole headline.

**Performance milestones work; production milestones don't.** Hits/Reaches Milestone 36,649 — those are box-office and streaming numbers. Production-stage milestones run 222 to 6,059. A number the reader can picture is the difference.

**Mask weak tags, name strong ones.** The same story swings enormously on whose name leads.

| Story | Named | Masked or re-led |
| --- | --: | --: |
| World War Z sequel | 1,228 | 13,391 (as "$540 Million Sci-Fi Action Hit") |
| Michelle Yeoh on Blade Runner | 471 | 6,948 (led with Ridley Scott) |
| Blu-ray in title vs slug | 9,968 | 12,785 (6.2% FR) |
| Sapkowski / author-led | 5,315 | franchise-led preferred |

**Check whether MW already ran it.** Caught four times in one session: the Ackles Tracker return (315, run twice), House of David (595), the WWZ sequel (1,228), the Harry Potter Hogwarts redesign (5,179). A saturated beat needs a new fact, not a new title.

## 7. Accuracy guardrails

These override the data. Every one of them cost traffic in exchange for being correct, and every one was the right call.

| Rejected | Used instead | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Condemned | Admits | The author hadn't condemned anything |
| Officially Moving Forward | Officially Addresses | Implies a greenlight that hadn't happened |
| Enters Production | Moving Forward | Nothing had entered production |
| "David Fincher's Zodiac" | dropped entirely | No such connection existed |
| Masterpiece | Classic | Critic scores didn't support it |
| Replaces | Eyed to Replace | Subject was only in talks |
| Greatest | Iconic | Unsupportable superlative |
| "Tragedy" (quoted) | dropped | 3 DC/A, and the most loaded word available |

**Genre relabelling is a grey zone.** Sci-Fi over Horror gains 5.9× and is defensible for a property with sci-fi elements. Action Thriller over Crime Thriller is a stretch when the studio itself calls it a drama. Judge each case; don't treat the traffic gain as automatic permission.

**Sensitive subject matter overrides everything.** On the Primetime review, the strongest available hook was the predator angle and it was the wrong one — the film's argument is about media power, not the men being caught.

## 8. Format-specific rules

**Reviews invert the News logic.** Mask the film, lead with the star, suffix "| Review". Star-led masked runs 3,123 S/A; the colon-subtitle format ('Film' Review: Verdict) runs 1,042 across 88 uses and is the desk's default. Superlatives run 583.

**Rotten Tomatoes scores** — qualitative adjectives beat numbers decisively.

| Descriptor | Uses | S/A | FR% | DV% | DC/A |
| --- | --: | --: | --: | --: | --: |
| Stellar / Solid / Rare / Killer | 24 | 14,684 | 8.3 | 79.2 | 7,692 |
| Officially | 35 | 9,542 | 11.4 | 62.9 | 5,519 |
| 100% / Perfect | 40 | 7,268 | 10.0 | 60.0 | 3,001 |
| "Score Revealed" | 26 | 5,099 | 7.7 | 30.8 | 512 |
| Bare numeric % | 60 | 4,231 | 31.7 | 48.3 | 2,032 |

The bare percentage is the most-used form (60 uses) and the weakest. "Score Officially Revealed" earns on search but contributes almost nothing to Discover.

**Physical media** — keep the format out of the headline. "Sets New Release" runs 29,828 at 3.2% failure and 85.5% DV. Blu-ray in the slug only beats Blu-ray in the title 12,785 to 9,968, with failure at 6.2% vs 28.6%. "4K" literal is the worst at 3,784.

**Routing.** Two placement findings worth acting on:

- Streaming runs RT stories at 14,202 S/A vs News at 7,230 — same format, same site.
- Flash News runs RT at 10,236 with 2.9% failure vs News at 6,530 with 20.1%. Score drops are timeliness plays; classify them as such.

## 9. Mechanics

**85-character hard cap** across site, SEO and SMO title fields.

**Trimming order.** Cut the untested word first, the redundant fact second, the genre modifier third. Never cut "Officially."

**Watch for redundancy across clauses.** "3-Part... After 3 Seasons" states the same fact twice, and "After N Seasons" is 3,076 S/A at 17.6% DV — the weakest tail in the file.

**Don't open with "First Look at"** — 3,683 S/A, 1,164 DC/A. Subject first, look phrase last: "...Debuts in First-Look Photos" (Johansson, 88,844) or "...in New Image" (Fraser, 98,280).

**Other mechanical notes:**

- Interrogative titles run −47% S/A.
- Em and en dashes appear in 0.3% of real titles, with 67% failure.
- Colon subtitles underperform outside Reviews, where they're the weak default.
- Quoting the show or movie title in the headline cuts S/A by more than half and Discover clicks by 2.5×.
- Passive trailing verbs bury the news — "Return Officially Confirmed" did 315.

## Pre-publish checklist

- [ ] Has MW run this story already? Check before writing anything.
- [ ] Is the subject a tag with real equity, or should it be masked?
- [ ] Is the payload concrete — can the reader picture what happened?
- [ ] Is "Officially" in there, and in front of the verb?
- [ ] Is the genre word the strongest accurate one?
- [ ] Any word from the avoid list?
- [ ] Is every claim supportable — no unearned superlatives, no implied greenlight?
- [ ] Under 85 characters?
- [ ] Correct content type — Flash News for timeliness plays, Streaming for streaming formats?

**When a story is genuinely weak, say so.** Several stories in this session had no strong accurate framing available, because the underlying event was soft. No title construction fixes a soft event. The right call there is a Niche News slot or a pass, not a better headline.
